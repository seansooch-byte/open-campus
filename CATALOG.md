# Open Campus: degree catalog research
Gathered 2026-11-17 from 13 research agents (11 colleges, degree structure, learning engines). Source material for SPEC.md v0.2. Facts marked unconfirmed by the agents stay unconfirmed.

## College of Business (accounting, finance, marketing, management, entrepreneurship, supply chain, business analytics)

Business is the largest bachelor's field in the US: 375,418 of 2,015,035 bachelor's degrees in 2021-22 (18.6%), per NCES Digest table 322.10. NCES Digest tables I opened report business as one aggregate row, so the ranking by sub-major below (general business/management, then accounting, finance, marketing, then the smaller majors) is from general knowledge; detailed counts are unconfirmed. The college works well for Open Campus because about two thirds of every business major is a shared common core (accounting, economics, statistics, law, management, marketing, information systems, finance, strategy). ETS's MFT Business outline names this core explicitly, so most courses can be written once and reused across all seven programs. The strongest free-or-cheap external anchors a learner can actually sit are: the five CLEP business exams at $97 each (Financial Accounting, Information Systems, Introductory Business Law, Principles of Management, Principles of Marketing); the DSST business titles; IRS Link & Learn VITA/TCE certification, which is free and backed by public-domain IRS material; INFORMS CAP-Essentials, which has no eligibility requirement; and ASCM CSCP, whose prerequisites were reportedly removed in December 2022. The prestige anchors cannot be sat by an Open Campus learner on Open Campus alone. The CPA exam (Core AUD, FAR, REG plus one Discipline: BAR, ISC or TCP) requires state-board education eligibility, and CFA requires its own eligibility. Open Campus can prepare a learner for their content but cannot make anyone eligible to sit them. Three findings change the spec. (1) Licensing: every current OpenStax business title, OpenStax Principles of Economics 3e and MIT OCW are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, not CC BY, so the spec's CC BY 4.0 content license cannot absorb the main business OER. Only Saylor (CC BY 3.0), Lyryx/Arnold and Kyle Intermediate Financial Accounting (CC BY 4.0, but IFRS/Canadian), OpenStax Introduction to Intellectual Property (CC BY), retired CC BY first editions, and US government works can be incorporated. (2) Currency: tax law and the CPA, CFA and ETS blueprints change on a schedule, so items need effective dates and a currency gate. (3) Verification: most of this college's answers are numbers, so items should carry an executable solution that the verifier runs, which is the fastest route to L3 here.

**First course to ship.** Financial Accounting, mapped by code to the CLEP Financial Accounting outline (General Topics, Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Statement of Cash Flows, Miscellaneous). Why: (1) Every program in this college requires it, and it is the first course of the CPA path toward FAR, so it is maximally reused. (2) A learner can actually sit the anchor for $97 at a proctored center, and an ACE score of 50 is worth 3 semester hours, giving a real external measurement. (3) Answers are deterministic (debits equal credits, statements tie), so the spreadsheet-ledger unit type and executable-solution verification can be built and proven here. That is the fastest path to L3 for the whole college. (4) There is an incorporable CC BY 3.0 base in Saylor BUS103, OpenStax Principles of Accounting Vol 1 (NC-SA) can serve as a linked reading, and EDGAR filings (public domain) provide real statements for exhibits. (5) It carries none of the tax-currency or licensure liability of REG and AUD, so it needs only an L2 sampling reviewer.

- **Hard parts:** The best-known OER for the core is NC-SA, so under a CC BY repo the core must be original prose that links to OpenStax rather than adapting it. The MFT cannot be sat by an individual, so the only individually measurable proof of the core is a stack of CLEP and DSST exams.

Blueprints organize content by Area, Group, Topic with weights; area weights not opened (in AICPA PDFs), unconfirmed; CPA licensure pathways after the 2025 UAA update: master's plus 1 year experience; bachelor's plus 30 credits (150) plus 1 year; bachelor's (120) plus 2 years. Open Campus satisfies none of the education requirement; CLEP Financial Accounting (General Topics 20-30%, Income Statement 20-30%, Balance Sheet 30-40%, Cash Flows 5-10%, Misc under 5%); IRS Link & Learn Taxes VITA/TCE certification (Basic, Advanced, Military, International, Foreign Student, Puerto Rico): free web training, the cheapest real proof of individual tax competence; IRS Special Enrollment Examination (Enrolled Agent): Part 1 Individuals, Part 2 Businesses, Part 3 Representation, Practices and Procedures; no degree required; vendor reportedly moved from Prometric to PSI in 2026 and fee reportedly $317 per part (secondary sources, unconfirmed); IMA CMA (two parts; page redirected, content and eligibility unconfirmed); ETS MFT Business Accounting section (~15%) as blueprint; AACSB 2026 Accounting Standards, A5 Digital Agility (curriculum guideline)
- **Course spine:** 1. Financial Accounting (CLEP Financial Accounting; FAR foundations); 2. Managerial Accounting (BAR cost and managerial); 3. Intermediate Accounting I: framework, revenue, receivables, inventory, PP&E (FAR); 4. Intermediate Accounting II: liabilities, leases, income taxes, pensions, equity, EPS (FAR); 5. Intermediate Accounting III: cash flows, changes and errors, disclosures (FAR); 6. Cost Accounting (BAR); 7. Accounting Information Systems and Internal Control (ISC; AUD internal control); 8. Federal Income Taxation of Individuals (REG; SEE Part 1; Link & Learn Advanced); 9. Taxation of Business Entities: C and S corporations, partnerships (REG; TCP; SEE Part 2); 10. Business Law for Accountants: contracts, agency, business structures (REG); 11. Auditing and Attestation (AUD); 12. Advanced Accounting: consolidations, foreign currency, partnerships (FAR, BAR); 13. Governmental and Not-for-Profit Accounting (FAR state and local government); 14. Accounting Data Analytics (BAR; AACSB A5); 15. Ethics, Professional Responsibility and Circular 230 (AUD, REG; SEE Part 3); 16. CPA Readiness capstone: full timed task-based simulation exams per section
- **Open sources:** Saylor BUS103 Introduction to Financial Accounting and BUS105 Managerial Accounting: CC BY 3.0 (Saylor-authored portions); Arnold and Kyle, Intermediate Financial Accounting Vol 1 and Vol 2 (Lyryx, 2016-2017): CC BY 4.0, BUT based on IFRS with Canadian ASPE content; a US GAAP adaptation is required before use for FAR; Lyryx Introduction to Financial Accounting: U.S. GAAP Adaptation 2021-A (Open Textbook Library listing; license not opened, unconfirmed); OpenStax Principles of Accounting Vol 1 and Vol 2: CC BY-NC-SA (link only); MIT OCW 15.501: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; Public domain primary law for tax: Internal Revenue Code, Treasury Regulations, IRS Publication 17 and other IRS publications, IRS Link & Learn modules, Circular 230; Public domain for audit and government: GAO Government Auditing Standards (Yellow Book); SEC Regulation S-X and S-K; EDGAR filings and XBRL data; Cite-only, copyrighted: FASB Accounting Standards Codification (free basic view), GASB standards, AICPA SAS/AU-C, COSO framework, AICPA blueprints. PCAOB standards are free to read; reuse license unconfirmed
- **Unit types:** spreadsheet ledger with debits-equal-credits and trial balance checks; CPA-style task-based simulation: exhibits (invoices, bank statements, trial balances), numeric entry, journal entry grid, multi-part scoring; authoritative research task restricted to public-domain sources (IRC, Treas. Reg., IRS pubs, Yellow Book, SEC rules) since FASB ASC cannot be reproduced; tax return lab: fill a mock Form 1040 or 1120-S from a fact pattern with computed checks; audit documentation workpaper exercise with rubric; time-versioned items keyed to tax year and blueprint version
- **Minimum capability level:** L2 for financial and managerial accounting, where answers are deterministic and machine-checkable. L1 for tax and audit. Tax figures (brackets, limits, phase-outs) change every year, and audit judgment is easy to state plausibly and wrongly. A wrong tax fact causes real harm to a learner who later prepares returns.
- **Human gates:** Named licensed CPA reviewer (license verifiable on a state board lookup) approves every REG, TCP and AUD item at L1, and samples FAR items at L2; Annual tax-currency sign-off each January and when the CPA blueprint rolls: every item tagged to a superseded tax year or blueprint is pulled until re-verified; Honesty wording: prepares for CPA exam content; does not satisfy any state board's education, experience or ethics exam requirement; Not tax advice disclaimer on all tax units; Circular 230 content reviewed by a CPA, EA or tax attorney; Trademark: CPA and the Uniform CPA Examination named nominatively; no claim of AICPA or NASBA affiliation
- **Hard parts:** There is no openly licensed US GAAP intermediate accounting text: the only CC BY intermediate text is IFRS-based. So FAR-level content must be original, written against the copyrighted FASB Codification, which can be cited but not quoted. The CPA's task-based simulations and annually changing tax content also need a rich exhibit format and a currency gate the spec does not yet have.

**Spec changes asked for.**

- Content license (section 12, decision 4): CC BY 4.0 is not 'the license most open textbooks use' in business. Every live OpenStax business title, OpenStax Principles of Economics 3e and MIT OCW are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; only Saylor (CC BY 3.0), Lyryx Arnold and Kyle (CC BY 4.0), OpenStax Intro to Intellectual Property (CC BY) and retired OpenStax first editions (CC BY) are incorporable. Add a per-source `license` and a `use: incorporated | adapted | linked` field, and a license-compatibility gate that refuses NC or SA text in a CC BY course. NC material also conflicts with a paid downstream such as AA, so add this as a factor in decision 4
- Anchor schema: generalize standards to `anchor { id, body, kind: sittable | blueprint_only | eligibility_gated | portfolio_internal, cost_usd, eligibility, blueprint_version, effective_from, effective_to }`. The ETS MFT is blueprint_only (institution administered), the CPA and CFA are eligibility_gated, CLEP, DSST and Link & Learn are sittable, and entrepreneurship portfolios are portfolio_internal
- Honesty wording (section 11): add 'Covers the content of <exam>. Does not satisfy <body>'s education, experience or eligibility requirements', required on every eligibility_gated anchor (CPA, CFA, CMA). Add 'Educational only; not tax, investment, accounting or legal advice' alongside the existing medical line. Add 'Not affiliated with or endorsed by AICPA, NASBA, CFA Institute, IMA, ASCM, INFORMS, ETS or College Board; marks used nominatively'
- Time-versioned content: add `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `tax_year`, `jurisdiction` (US federal, a state, IFRS vs US GAAP) on items, and a currency gate that pulls items when their standard's version or tax year rolls. Add a scheduled 'standards rollover' step to the release playbook alongside the model-release run (CPA blueprint every January; tax law yearly)
- Computed answers: allow an item to carry an executable solution (code or spreadsheet formula) plus a numeric tolerance. The Verifier must run it, and a mismatch fails the item. This is how quantitative business content earns L3
- Generalize the clinical gate (course contract item 4, principle 9) to `regulated_domain: clinical | tax | audit | investment | legal` with a credentialed-reviewer record (credential type, issuing body, verifiable license lookup, e.g. a state CPA license). Tax and audit items start at L1
- New unit types in the course contract: ledger, task_based_simulation (with exhibits), spreadsheet_model, research_task (public-domain primary sources only), case_memo (rubric), market_or_supply_simulation (deterministic seed), code_notebook (hidden tests), portfolio, oral_pitch
- Copyright note for standards: CPA blueprints, FASB ASC, GASB, AICPA SAS, COSO and the ASCM body of knowledge are copyrighted. Map by code and our own wording only, and keep research tasks to public-domain sources (IRC, Treasury Regs, IRS pubs, GAO Yellow Book, SEC rules)
- Programs schema: add `shared_core` so the common business core is one set of courses referenced by seven programs, and let a program carry several anchors (a stack of CLEP and DSST exams plus a capstone) rather than one exit exam
- Datasets as first-class records: `datasets/` with source, license and retrieval date (EDGAR, Census, BLS public domain), checked by the originality and license gate

## Law and Public Service (pre-law and LSAT, legal studies and paralegal, criminal justice, public administration)

Ranked by graduates. NCES Digest table 322.10 (2021-22 bachelor's): Homeland security, law enforcement and firefighting 56,901; Public administration and social services 33,429 (this family includes social work, and the count for public administration alone is unconfirmed); Legal professions and studies 4,444 (paralegal is mostly an associate degree or certificate, and those counts are unconfirmed). Pre-law has few graduates. The ABA recommends no undergraduate major for law school (ABA position, seen in search results only). Even so, the LSAT is the hardest and best-measured anchor in this college. Free learners can measure themselves on four LSAC Official PrepTests that come free on LawHub.

Three facts shape this college.
(1) Several anchors cannot be sat by a free learner. The ETS Major Field Test in Criminal Justice is given only by a program, with a proctor. NALA CP has eligibility categories. PACE requires experience. California's FYLSX is for registered law students in an unaccredited school or the Law Office Study program. The spec therefore needs an "anchor kind" and a "learner can sit" field.
(2) The best legal open texts carry NonCommercial or ShareAlike licenses: H2O is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, many CALI eLangdell books are BY-NC-SA, and Johnson's Torts is BY-SA 4.0. They cannot be absorbed into CC BY 4.0 content. Public domain primary law is the real open corpus: court opinions (the Caselaw Access Project data has been unrestricted since March 2024 and is mirrored in CourtListener), the US Code, the CFR, and the federal rules.
(3) Law varies by jurisdiction and changes between model releases. Every legal item therefore needs a jurisdiction and a "law as of" date, plus a currency audit that runs on the calendar, not only on model releases.

Honesty boundary. Open Campus may say it teaches legal reasoning and introductory doctrine and prepares for the LSAT, DSST, CLEP and paralegal exams. It may never say or imply that it is a law school, a JD equivalent, a path to bar admission, or a source of legal advice.
- California's Law Office Study program requires a supervising attorney, at least 18 hours a week (four years per secondary sources), the FYLSX, the MPRE and the bar exam. Open Campus content can supplement that program but never counts toward it.
- Which other states allow law office study is unconfirmed.

Unauthorized practice of law (UPL). A tutor that applies law to a learner's own facts risks UPL. ABA Model Rule 5.5 and state rules apply. Per secondary sources, a 2005 Oregon opinion found that legal information crosses into practice when it is applied to a specific person's problem. Tutor packs need a measured UPL refusal bar.

**First course to ship.** LSAT Logical Reasoning and Reading Comprehension, as one course mapped to the LSAT's two scored section types and its question-type taxonomy.

Why this course first:
- It is the MCAT pattern again: a hard external exam the project does not write, with a free official measurement (four LawHub PrepTests), so learners can prove the method on their own.
- It carries no UPL risk, no jurisdiction variance and no law-currency decay, so it needs no attorney gate.
- It reuses the critical-reading kernel already built for MCAT CARS.
- Its weakness: there is no open item source, so every item is original. The ambiguity verifier built here becomes reusable infrastructure for every college.

Second course: Introduction to Criminal Justice, mapped to DSST Criminal Justice. It serves the largest graduate population and earns real credit through an existing route. It needs the jurisdiction tag and a named reviewer, which is why it comes second.

## College of Computing (computer science, software engineering, data science and machine learning, information technology, cybersecurity)

Computing is the one college where grading can be fully automatic. Biggest risk: models are strong enough to author this college at L3, so the real hard parts are (a) legal and ethical gates on offensive-security labs, (b) keeping autograder tests from being gamed or leaked, and (c) honestly saying that certificates and portfolios, not credit, are what employers see.

(2) The best open text for it carries a clean CC BY 4.0 license (OpenStax Introduction to Python Programming), so there is no NC-SA segregation problem. (3) Pyodide grading in the browser proves the zero-server code-runner unit type that the whole college depends on. (4) Correctness is machine-checkable, so the autograder becomes an objective verification lane that clears L3 bars fastest. (5) Planted-defect evals are natural here: wrong reference solutions and weak test suites are easy to generate and score with mutation testing. (6) Demand is the largest of any computing course.

## Engineering (mechanical, electrical, civil, chemical, biomedical, industrial)

Engineering fits the spec better than most colleges because it has one free-standing, sit-able national exam per discipline: the NCEES FE exam (110 questions, computer based, about 6 hours, $225, year-round at Pearson centers). Each discipline's spec gives numbered knowledge areas with question ranges, so it can serve directly as `standards/` data by code. The specs I opened (Mechanical, Electrical and Computer, Civil, Chemical, Industrial and Systems, Other Disciplines) are all marked effective July 2020. NCEES began a knowledge content review in January 2026, so a revision is coming; the effective date is unconfirmed. ABET EAC Criterion 3 (outcomes 1 to 7) and Criterion 5 (at least 30 credits of math and basic science, at least 45 credits of engineering topics, a culminating major design experience) serve as the program-level framework anchor. You can be measured against them but cannot sit them. NCES Digest table 325.47 for 2020-21 gives bachelor's degrees as mechanical 36,224, electrical 16,914, civil 15,051 and chemical 9,986. Biomedical and industrial counts are unconfirmed (I did not open a table for them). Four findings change the spec. (1) Most of engineering can be checked by machine: numeric answers with units can be verified by a reference solver, which is a verification lane that is not a model and lets several courses reach L3 early. (2) Physical labs and team design (ABET outcomes 5 and 6) cannot be delivered free and static, so the spec needs an honest experiment-mode field rather than pretending. (3) Civil, chemical, power and biomedical content touches life safety and copyrighted design codes (ACI, AISC, ASCE 7, NEC, ISO 13485), which need a new gate. (4) The license premise in spec section 12 is wrong for this college: OpenStax Calculus, University Physics, Chemistry 2e and Introductory Statistics 2e, and all of MIT OCW, are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (checked 2026-11-17), and some engineering texts are NoDerivatives, so a CC BY library cannot absorb them.

**First course to ship.** Engineering Statics. It is an FE knowledge area in four of the six programs (Mechanical area 6, 9-14 questions; Civil area 4, 8-12; Other Disciplines area 8, 9-14, which covers BME; and the BME spine's combined Statics and Mechanics of Materials). Every item reduces to equilibrium equations, so a reference solver verifies the answer key without any model. That makes it the first place to prove the oracle-lane idea and reach L3. It needs no physical lab, carries no life-safety or code-copyright exposure, and it is the prerequisite for dynamics, mechanics of materials and structural analysis. Mechanics Map (share-alike) and Engineering Statics: Open and Interactive exist as open references to link, while our CC BY prose stays original. Engineering Economics is the runner-up: it appears in every FE discipline and is pure spreadsheet math.

## College of Health Professions (nursing, public health, health administration, nutrition, allied health, pre-med, preclinical medicine)

Health professions is the second largest bachelor's field in the US: 263,765 bachelor's degrees in 2021-22 out of 2,015,035 (NCES Digest table 322.10). At the associate level it had 177,413 degrees, and 81,611 of those were "Nursing, registered nurse and other" (table 321.10). I could not find a bachelor's-level breakdown by subfield, so the ordering below (nursing first, then health administration, public health, pre-med and health sciences, nutrition, exercise science, other allied health, preclinical medicine) follows the associate data and is otherwise unconfirmed. The defining fact of this college is that almost every license it prepares for is gated on enrollment or supervised hours that cannot happen online. NCLEX eligibility is set by each nursing regulatory body (NCSBN keeps no list). USMLE Step 1 requires enrollment in or graduation from an LCME, COCA or ECFMG-eligible school. ARRT requires a recognized program plus clinical competencies. NBCOT requires an ACOTE program. CPH requires a CEPH program, or a bachelor's plus 5 years of public health work. CHES requires a degree plus 25 qualifying credits. RDN requires a graduate degree, ACEND coursework and at least 1,000 supervised practice hours (secondary sources; the CDR page was not retrieved). So the spec must separate "standards we map to" from "exams a free learner can actually sit". The sittable ones are CLEP, DSST, AP, the MCAT and the AAPC CPC, which needs no degree per AAPC's prerequisites page. A second major finding changes the license plan. OpenStax's help center now says all OpenStax textbooks are CC BY-NC-SA, and the nursing, A&P 2e and Nutrition pages show CC BY-NC-SA. That is incompatible with the spec's planned CC BY 4.0 content license. The best CC BY 4.0 base for this college is Open RN (WisTech Open: 20+ nursing texts, 30+ screen-based simulations, 25 VR scenario plans), plus US federal works such as the CDC Principles of Epidemiology (SS1978).

**First course to ship.** Population Health I: Epidemiology and Biostatistics. It maps by code to CPH Domains 1 (Data, Analytics and Informatics) and 10 (Health Outcomes and Population Perspectives), the CHES Nine Areas where they overlap, and the MCAT statistics codes. Its measurable anchor is DSST Principles of Statistics, which anyone can sit. Why ship it first: (1) It is non-clinical, so it can ship at L2 or L3 without first building the licensed-reviewer bench that every nursing, nutrition and medicine course needs. (2) Its spine source is a federal work (CDC SS1978, Principles of Epidemiology, 3rd ed.) plus public datasets, so it avoids the OpenStax NC-SA problem. (3) Answers are computable, so a code runner and calculators can verify items mechanically, which is the strongest possible verifier. (4) It is reused by nearly every program in the college: nursing population health, health administration, nutrition research methods, exercise science research, pre-med statistics and preclinical biostatistics. (5) It forces the college's one new unit type that other colleges also want (data labs on real data) before the hardest one (NCJMM case studies). Ship nursing second, starting with Pharmacology and Dosage Calculation on Open RN Nursing Pharmacology (CC BY 4.0), once an RN reviewer is named.

## Mathematics and Statistics (pure and applied mathematics, statistics, actuarial science, operations research)

NCES Digest table 322.10 lists 26,212 bachelor's degrees in "Mathematics and statistics" for 2021-22. I could not find a national count for each CIP code, so the program order below (general mathematics, then statistics, applied mathematics, actuarial science, operations research) is my estimate and is unconfirmed. This college is the easiest place in the university to check answers by machine. Calculus, linear algebra, probability and statistics answers can be checked by a computer algebra system, by recomputing the number, or in a code runner. Proofs written in Lean can be checked by its kernel. That makes it the best college for testing whether the Drafter can reach L3. The two hardest problems are licensing and proofs. (1) Most of the best open math books are licensed NC, SA, ND or GFDL. MIT OCW is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. OpenStax Calculus is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Book of Proof 3rd edition is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Judson's Abstract Algebra is GFDL 1.2+. OpenIntro is CC BY-SA 3.0. None of these can be adapted into a CC BY 4.0 record. The spec needs a source-license field that separates "adapt" from "read alongside". (2) A proof written in ordinary language cannot be checked mechanically. That needs a new proof unit with three kinds of checking (Lean kernel, rubric plus human, rubric plus model) and an honest label on each. The external anchors a learner can actually sit are strong in actuarial science (SOA P, FM, FAM, SRM; CAS MAS-I and MAS-II) and in early calculus and statistics (CLEP Calculus, CLEP College Algebra, AP Calculus, AP Statistics). They are weak in upper-division pure math, where only the GRE Mathematics Subject Test and the ETS Major Field Test (an institutional test) exist. Those programs also have to anchor to curriculum guidelines: MAA CUPM 2015 and ASA 2014.

**First course to ship.** Calculus I, anchored to CLEP Calculus (limits about 10%, differential about 50%, integral about 40%; ACE recommends a credit score of 50 for 4 semester hours) and to AP Calculus AB. Reasons: (1) It is a prerequisite for every program in this college and for engineering, physics, economics and actuarial science, so its learner base is the largest. (2) Every answer can be checked for equivalence by a computer algebra system, so the Verifier has a machine lane that cannot hallucinate. That makes this the safest course for proving that the Drafter and Planner can reach L3, which the spec's Stage 4 needs anyway. (3) CLEP is a cheap external exam a learner can actually sit for credit. The second course should be Probability for SOA Exam P. It is the cheapest real professional exam in the college ($275), and it opens both the actuarial and statistics programs.

**First course to ship.** College Composition I, mapped to CLEP College Composition (sittable by any adult) and coded against the CWPA Five Knowledge Domains 4.0, with AP English Language as a secondary anchor. Why:
1. Every degree in every college requires it, so it has the widest reach.
2. Its main source is CC BY 4.0 (OpenStax Writing Guide with Handbook), so it can be remixed into the record without a license conflict.

## College of Education (teacher preparation, early childhood, special education, instructional design)

Education is mid-sized and mostly graduate. NCES Digest 2023 counts 89,410 bachelor's degrees in Education for 2021-22, out of 2,015,035 in all fields (Table 322.10). It counts 151,707 education master's degrees, out of 880,249 (Table 323.10). I could not confirm NCES counts per subfield, so the program order below is a judgment (unconfirmed). The knowledge half of teacher preparation fits the spec well, because anyone who pays the testing body can sit the licensure exams. Examples are Praxis 5001 (subtests 5002 to 5005), 5025, 5354, PLT 5621 to 5624, 5205, Core 5752 and ParaPro 1755, plus Pearson Foundations of Reading 890 and CLEP Introduction to Educational Psychology. The clinical half cannot be delivered. A state license requires completing a state-approved program that includes supervised student teaching. CAEP 2022 Standard R2 covers this in three parts: R2.1 partnerships, R2.2 clinical educators and R2.3 clinical experiences. NCTQ recommends at least 10 weeks of student teaching. State rules vary: Colorado 800 clock hours, Nebraska 14 weeks, Oregon 15 weeks, Florida no minimum (NCTQ pages dated 2011 to 2017). edTPA (Pearson) also needs a real placement, and so does PPAT (ETS) as far as I know (unconfirmed). Open Campus can prepare a learner for every written exam and offer simulated practice. It must say it cannot license anyone or replace student teaching. One narrow exception is documented: per NCTQ, Wisconsin exempts ABCTE alternate-route candidates from student teaching (the page is dated, so current status is unconfirmed). Licensing is the other problem. Many of the best education OER are NonCommercial: IRIS Center is CC BY-NC-ND, Design for Learning is CC BY-NC, and Open Oregon Foundations of Education and the Lombardi special education texts are CC BY-NC-SA. None of these can be remixed into CC BY content. Federal publications such as the WWC practice guides are likely public domain (unconfirmed). Exam outlines from ETS and CAEP are copyrighted, so they can only be referenced by code.

**First course to ship.** Educational Psychology, mapped to CLEP Introduction to Educational Psychology (100 questions, 90 min, scaled 20 to 80) and to the PLT 5621 to 5624 codes. It is the best first course for five reasons. (1) It has a credit-by-exam anchor any learner can sit, which none of the licensure-only courses have. (2) It has a fully CC BY base text (Seifert and Sutton, Educational Psychology, 2nd ed.) that can be remixed under the spec's content license. (3) It reuses Pre-Health psychology content (learning, memory, development, motivation) almost directly, so it is the cheapest course to finish. (4) It is a required course in every program in this college, including Instructional Design course 2. (5) It involves no minors, no state law and no clinical placement, so it can ship at L2 without new gates. The weights a study guide attributes to College Board (individual differences 17%, cognitive 15%, development 15%, testing 12%, behaviorist 11%, motivation 10%) are unconfirmed and must be checked against the official fact sheet before the coverage map is built.

## Arts, Design and Architecture

Scale: NCES Digest table 322.10 gives 90,241 bachelor's degrees in Visual and Performing Arts (CIP 50) and 9,462 in Architecture and related services (CIP 04) in 2021-22. NCES does not publish a national subfield split. DataUSA reports 10,676 graphic design degrees awarded in 2024, all levels. The order below (studio art, graphic design, film, music, architecture, product design, interior design, art history) is therefore unconfirmed as a ranking.

This college breaks the draft spec's assumption that every course has a sittable exam. Only five kinds of external anchor exist here:
- AP exams anyone can register for: Music Theory, Art History, and the 2-D, 3-D and Drawing portfolios.
- CLEP Humanities, where the arts are 50% of the exam.
- DSST Art of the Western World.
- Licensing exams: NCARB ARE 5.0 for architecture and NCIDQ for interior design. Both are gated by education and experience rules that Open Campus cannot satisfy.
- Accreditor curriculum standards that a learner cannot sit: NASAD, NASM and NAAB. The NAAB 2020 Conditions have Student Criteria SC.1 to SC.6 and Program Criteria PC.1 to PC.7.

So the spec needs a weaker anchor class, "accreditor-guideline", with honest wording. It also needs the portfolio as a first-class unit, with every rubric row marked as judgeable by a model or reserved for humans.

What a model can judge: formal and technical criteria such as notation and part-writing rules, typographic hierarchy, colour contrast ratios, grid alignment, file and print specifications, code compliance arithmetic, structural calculations, and whether process documentation is present.

What it cannot own: originality, voice, aesthetic quality, whether a sustained investigation is genuine inquiry, physical craft seen only in photos, and anything that counts as evidence toward licensure.

Licensing is the other blocker. Most of the best open sources here are NonCommercial-ShareAlike (Smarthistory, MIT OCW, Moving Pictures). Those cannot be merged into CC BY 4.0 content, so the spec needs a per-item license field and a compatibility gate. Media rights for images, recordings and film clips need their own gate.

CIP codes are given from the CIP 2020 taxonomy from memory; I did not re-open the CIP site this session, so treat them as unconfirmed.

**First course to ship.** Music Theory I and II with Aural Skills, mapped by unit and skill code to AP Music Theory.

Why it goes first:
- It is the only course in this college with a hard external exam that a free learner can sit, with a confirmed format: 75 MC questions, 7 written free-response items, 2 sight-singing items.
- It has a CC BY-SA 4.0 primary text, Open Music Theory v2.
- Most of its outputs are machine-checkable without a model judging taste: a MusicXML part-writing rule checker, dictation graded against synthesized audio, and sight-singing scored by in-browser pitch detection.
- It runs fully static at zero marginal cost. It avoids recording rights by rendering our own audio from public-domain scores.
- It forces three generalizations every other course here needs: a non-prose answer runner, an audio unit, and a rubric whose rows are tagged model-judgeable versus human-only.

Caveat: the CC BY-SA text cannot be merged into a CC BY 4.0 corpus. The spec must allow per-item licenses before it ships.

**Spec changes asked for.**

- Anchors: add a typed `anchor_kind` enum: exam_sittable_free_learner, exam_gated_by_eligibility (ARE, NCIDQ), credit_by_exam (CLEP, DSST, AP), institutional_test (ETS MFT), accreditor_guideline (NASAD, NASM, NAAB, CIDA, CAA), portfolio_standard (AP Art and Design), published_standard (WCAG, ADA). - Licensing: add per-item `license` and `source_license` fields and a license-compatibility gate. Section 12 assumes CC BY 4.0 everywhere, but the key sources here are CC BY-SA (Open Music Theory, Sachant) or CC BY-NC-SA (Smarthistory, MIT OCW, Moving Pictures). Either partition the corpus by license or rewrite those sources as original text with links.
- Media rights gate: every image, audio file, score edition and video clip carries `rights_basis` (PD-age, PD-gov, CC0, CC-BY, CC-BY-SA, own-render) with a source URL. No fair-use reliance. A positive control plants a copyrighted image.
- New unit types in the schema: portfolio (artifacts plus process plus written inquiry), studio_brief (constraint, materials, time box), crit (rubric review), notation (MusicXML answer plus rule checker), audio (dictation, listening ID, sight-singing with local pitch detection), timecoded_annotation (film and score), plan_check (accessibility and egress over learner drawings), cad_check, calc_sheet, case_brief.

## degree-structure

Breadth can be built on standards the project does not write. NCES's CIP 2020 is the catalog spine: 50 two-digit series, about 464 active four-digit codes and about 2,170 active six-digit codes, counted from NCES's CIPCode2020.csv. NCES Digest Table 318.30 (2021-22) gives 2,015,035 bachelor's degrees. The 60 six-digit CIP majors listed below account for 68.2% of them: the top 40 cover 61.0% and the top 50 cover 65.1%, and all 60 codes were checked against the CIP file. A bachelor's degree can be stated honestly as 120 credits. 34 CFR 600.2 defines a semester credit hour as at least 1 hour of instruction plus 2 hours of outside work per week for about 15 weeks, so about 45 learner-hours per credit and about 5,400 hours for a degree. UoPeople (120 credits = 40 courses of 3 credits, at least 6 capstone credits) and WGU (about 120 competency units, each roughly a credit hour) both fit that shape. Where no exam exists, these frameworks can anchor courses: the DQP (Lumina/NILOA, 2014), the 16 to 17 AAC&U VALUE rubrics (AAC&U calls them an open educational resource), Tuning USA (pilot started 2009), ACM/IEEE-CS/AAAI CS2023, and APA Guidelines 3.0 (5 goals, 2023). A 42-credit general education core maps almost course for course onto CLEP's 34 exams and DSST's 40 exams. The precedents point one way: providers that went broad (Saylor, Modern States) went broad only at the gen-ed layer, where external exams exist, and providers that grant degrees (UoPeople, WGU, OSSU, MicroMasters) completed one program at a time. My inference: list every major cheaply and in the open, build the shared core to depth first, then take programs to depth one at a time, and show learners a per-program tier. One finding that affects the spec's license plan: OpenStax's own CMS today lists 70 of its 74 English-language live titles as CC BY-NC-SA and only 4 as CC BY. Most current OpenStax books therefore cannot be absorbed into a CC BY record. They can be linked as outside readings, and older editions released under CC BY 4.0 may still be reused under that license (unconfirmed per edition).

### Credit hour and honest workload

Define workload in learner-hours from the federal credit-hour definition, and state it on every course and program page. Do not claim credit; claim an equivalent workload.

**Spec text.** Workload. A course states its size in credit-equivalents, where 1 credit-equivalent = 45 learner-hours (the US federal definition of a semester credit hour, 34 CFR 600.2: at least one hour of instruction and two hours of outside work per week for about fifteen weeks). A 3-credit-equivalent course is about 135 hours. A bachelor's-equivalent program is 120 credit-equivalents, about 5,400 hours. These numbers describe effort, not credit: Open Campus grants no credit.

**Evidence.** 34 CFR 600.2 (Cornell LII text): a credit hour 'reasonably approximates not less than one hour of classroom or direct faculty instruction and a minimum of two hours of out-of-class student work each week for approximately fifteen weeks for one semester or trimester hour of credit, or ten to twelve weeks for one quarter hour'. The definition lets institutions count other kinds of academic work as equivalent. WGU: about 120 CUs for an undergraduate degree, and 'CUs are basically the same as a credit-hour'. UoPeople catalog 2025/26: 120 credit hours, 40 courses, each 3 credits over 9 weeks.

### Composition of a bachelor's program

Give every program the same five blocks with fixed credit budgets, so that a program file is a fill-in-the-blanks record rather than a design exercise.

**Spec text.** Program. A bachelor's-equivalent program is a file programs/<cip>.json with: cip (6-digit CIP 2020 code), title, award: 'bachelor-equivalent', credits: 120, and five blocks. ### How precedents sequenced breadth

## unit-engines

Recommends 11 learning engines plus one pluggable unit-type contract, so a course in any major can use more than prose, cards, MCQs and Lab lessons. Every engine below runs in the browser on static hosting. Each unit type ships with a reference answer and a check that runs headless in CI. A unit is accepted only when its reference passes, its starter fails, and planted wrong answers are rejected. That makes "a machine can verify this unit" the bar for each capability level. Findings that change the draft: (1) Do not use WebContainers. StackBlitz says production use in a commercial, for-profit setting needs a license, and its page does not say whether the core is open source. Use Pyodide (MPL-2.0), sql.js (MIT), or plain JS in a Worker instead. (2) Browser Lean is not ready for the default path. lean4web runs Lean on a server. xeus-lean runs fully in the browser but describes itself as a playground and needs about 2 GB per tab, with Mathlib at 280 MB to 1.7 GB compressed. So check proofs in CI and give learners a static replay. (3) PhET's CC BY 4.0 grant explicitly covers only simulations released before 2026-03-29; I could not confirm the terms for later ones, so pin pre-cutoff versions. (4) The OpenStax accounting texts are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Whether NonCommercial-ShareAlike sources can enter the record is a LICENSE-CONTENT decision for Sean. (5) HyperFormula is GPLv3 or commercial and webR binaries are GPL-3, so record a copyleft policy per engine. Suggested build order, chosen to unlock the most majors soonest: math input, then code runner, then rubric-graded open response, then notebooks, then ledger and spreadsheet, then simulation, then chemistry structure, then law brief, then language audio, then portfolio, then formal proof.

### Course contract: pluggable unit types (exact spec text)

Replace contract items 2 and 3 with a unit-type registry. Every non-prose unit is an instance of a registered type. Each type declares a schema, a pinned runtime, a check script and the minimum capability level needed to author it. course-check dispatches each unit to its type's check. A new engine joins by adding a type folder, not by editing the contract. The capability ledger gets one row per (role, unit type), not one per role.

**Spec text.** 2. **Typed units.** A course is built from units. Every unit declares `type`, and the type must be registered in `packages/unit-types/<type>/`. A registered type supplies: `schema` (a Zod schema for the unit's files), `runtime` (the exact engine name, version and content hash the learner's browser loads; no runtime may need a server on the default path), `check` (a headless script that course-check runs in CI), `license` (the engine's license and its copyleft obligations) and `min_level` (the lowest ledger level at which a model may author the type without per-item human review). Every unit that can be checked by machine ships a reference answer. Its check must show three things: the reference passes, the starter or empty response fails, and every planted wrong answer listed in the unit is rejected. Units that cannot be checked by machine (rubric, portfolio) instead ship anchor responses with human-assigned scores, and the grader lane must reproduce those scores to the type's agreement threshold. A topic declares the unit types its standard codes require (`requires: [code, math, notebook, ...]`). Coverage is reported per standard code and per required type. Lab lessons are the registered type `lab`. Prose, cards and question sets are the registered types `prose`, `cards` and `mcq`. The ledger records a level per (role, unit type), and a model may author a type unsupervised only when its ledger level for that pair meets the type's `min_level`.

**Evidence.** Generalizes the current contract (SPEC.md section 5 items 2 and 3, and lab-check as a gate) to N engines. Engine-specific facts are in the entries below.

### Code runner with auto-graded tests

### Math input with symbolic checking

MathLive (MIT) for input and Cortex Compute Engine (MIT) for checking. Use `isIdenticallyEqual`, which expands, simplifies and samples at random points; `isEqual` does not simplify and returns undefined for (x+1)^2 vs x^2+2x+1. CI cross-checks with SymPy under Pyodide, so a sampled true is backed by a second engine. Majors: mathematics, statistics, physics, all engineering (FE exam), chemistry, economics, finance, CS. Also every credit-by-exam calculus and algebra outline. Data format: `answer` as MathJSON plus LaTeX; `mode` (equivalent, numeric with tolerance, or form-required such as factored or simplified); `domain` for sampling; `wrong_answers` listing common errors, each with feedback. Machine gate: the reference is equivalent to itself in both engines, the two engines agree on the reference, and every listed wrong answer is rejected by both. Minimum capability: L2, rising to L3 once the two-engine agreement gate is in place. Build this engine first: it unlocks the most majors for the least work.

**Spec text.** Unit type `math`: runtime is MathLive plus Compute Engine (browser), and SymPy on Pyodide (CI only). The check confirms that the reference answer passes in both engines under the declared mode, and that every entry in `wrong_answers` is rejected by both. If the engines disagree, the unit fails. min_level L2.

**Evidence.** Compute Engine guide: isSame is structural; isEqual says "No expansion, no simplification and no sampling is attempted"; isIdenticallyEqual uses expansion, simplification and sampling, and "a true from sampling is strong evidence but not a formal proof". MathLive and Compute Engine READMEs: MIT.

### Rubric-graded essay and open response (learner's own Claude)

**Evidence.** Design within SPEC.md section 7.2 (L5 = graded open responses that count as evidence) and section 10 (course packs). No external engine is needed.
