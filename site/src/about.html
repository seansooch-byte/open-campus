About Open Campus

College still pays. Its price is the problem.

The price of college has outrun prices overall for four decades, and millions pay part of it without finishing. The knowledge itself now costs almost nothing to deliver. Open Campus gives it away.

Open Campus is a free, open library of university courses for 60 majors, written as data, checked item by item, and tied to exams it does not write.

A college charges for knowledge, teaching, proof and experience at once. Colleges spend $8,000 to $20,000 per student per year on instruction alone;4 serving a static course page to one more learner costs effectively nothing.5 Open Campus separates the knowledge from the price and leaves the proof to bodies that already grant it.

How a course is built

  1. 1Start from an outside standardEach course maps by code to an exam or curriculum it does not write, such as a CLEP outline or a licensing blueprint.
  2. 2Write it as open dataLessons, worked examples, flashcards and practice questions with an explanation for every choice, under CC BY 4.0.
  3. 3Check every item twiceThe checker is never the writer, and every item records both. Clinical and licensure items need a named human reviewer.
  4. 4Re-check with every new modelEach better AI model audits the whole library again, and the error rate it finds is published.

What it is not

Open Campus is not accredited, grants no degrees or credit, and is not affiliated with any testing or licensing body. Learners can earn credit through credit-by-exam programs such as CLEP ($97 an exam; each college decides what it accepts).6 Pre-medicine is offered separately by AdmissionsAcademy.
